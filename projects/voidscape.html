A first-person roguelike with procedurally generated dungeons, craftable weapons, and more than fifty gameplay-modifying mechanics. The part worth talking about is what happens after you win.

The problem

Every roguelike I played had the same failure: the god run. The item drops line up, damage goes exponential, and the boss you were supposed to fear dies in a single shot. The run you waited ten hours for is the least interesting one you'll play, because the game stopped asking anything of you at the exact moment it should have asked the most.

Stat scaling causes it. If power is a number and enemies are a number, one of them eventually runs away from the other, and no amount of tuning fixes it — it just moves where the break happens.

What I built

  • Two loops per run. The normal run ends. Then a second, infinitely scalable run begins, and that one escalates on execution rather than on stats — the difficulty comes from what the encounter demands of the player, not from bigger health bars.
  • A skill tree fed by the second loop. Points earned there persist, so the long-term progression is attached to the part of the game that tests you.
  • Boons in the Binding of Isaac mould — run-defining modifiers that combine in ways I didn't explicitly author.
  • Craftable weapons that survive between runs, so building something is a decision with consequences past the current session.

What it cost

Fifty interacting modifiers means the combinations are not testable by hand. Most of the work wasn't writing mechanics, it was making them compose without producing states I never anticipated — and accepting that some of those states are the reason the game is interesting.

What I'd change

It's playable through two bosses and unfinished past that. If I picked it back up, I'd cut the modifier count hard and spend the time on the second loop instead — that's the idea, and the first loop is mostly there to set it up.
