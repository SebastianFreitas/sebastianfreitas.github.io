# Phase 9 research: the found suit

A wide, short, block-shaped creature in a frock coat, trousers and a gibus
that were all cut for someone else: a taller, narrower man, now dead or
pawned out. Short web pass, September 2026.

## Digest

- **Rag Fair, Houndsditch / Petticoat Lane.** East London's old-clothes
  exchange, where the middle and upper classes' cast-offs, and dead men's
  wardrobes, came down the ladder to the poor: "from the richest in the land
  to the tattered garments of poverty". Pieces came from different owners,
  so outfits were assembled, never matched.
  [mernick.org.uk](https://www.mernick.org.uk/thhol/ragfair1.html),
  [Spitalfields Life](https://spitalfieldslife.com/2011/07/17/at-the-rag-fair-in-houndsditch/),
  [victorianlondon.org](https://www.victorianlondon.org/clothing/secondhand.htm)
- **Monmouth Street, "the burial-place of the fashions".** In Sketches by
  Boz, Dickens reads a dead man's whole life off the suits hanging in one
  shop, and Cruikshank's 1839 plate hangs coats, hats and boots off the
  shopfronts like empty people. A found garment still carries the shape of
  its first owner.
  [Victorian Web](https://victorianweb.org/art/illustration/cruikshank/boz9.html)
- **Pawnshops.** Working families pawned the Sunday suit on Monday and got
  it back on payday, week after week, so good cloth wore at the folds and
  creases from handling as much as from wear.
  [Victorian Web](https://victorianweb.org/history/london/pawnbrokers.html),
  [Visitor's Guide](https://visitvictorianengland.com/2019/05/24/victorian-pawnbrokers/)
- **Mudlarks (Mayhew).** Fustian coats, greasy canvas trousers roughly
  patched, trousers tucked up or worn away to the knee, mud up to the hem.
  The bottom of the garment is where the dirt and wear pile up.
  [Spitalfields Life](https://spitalfieldslife.com/2010/09/29/the-life-of-a-mudlark-1861/),
  [RMG](https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/maritime-history/library-archive/mudlarks-murky-world-londons-river-scavengers)
- **Phiz and Jingle: the classic tell.** Dickens's Jingle wears a faded
  swallowtail dress coat made for a shorter man, with bare wrist showing
  between glove and cuff. Phiz and the later illustrators keep that wrist
  gap as the whole sign of shabby gentility: good cut, wrong body, old fashion.
  [Victorian Web: Furniss's Jingle](https://victorianweb.org/art/illustration/furniss/215.html),
  [Phiz, Pickwick](https://victorianweb.org/art/illustration/phiz/pickwick/12.html)
- **Grandville's dressed animals (1842).** The satire works because
  human tailoring is stretched over a body that is not human: the coat keeps
  its cut, the animal's shape pushes against it at the belly and shoulders,
  and the tails fall wherever that shape drops them.
  [Met Museum](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/335057),
  [Princeton Graphic Arts](https://graphicarts.princeton.edu/2014/06/01/des-animaux)
- **Mervyn Peake.** Gormenghast figures are exaggerated silhouettes (the
  bulk of Swelter, the spikiness of Flay) with the clothes drawn as heavy
  folds that hang off the body or strain against it; shadow sits in the folds.
  [Flashbak](https://flashbak.com/mervyn-peake-illustrations-gormenghast-trilogy-1970-400002/),
  [Frieze](https://www.frieze.com/article/sinister-sensuality-mervyn-peakes-drawings)
- **The gibus.** A collapsible silk top hat (Gibus brothers, Paris, patents
  from 1837). Inside, a crown hoop and a brim hoop are joined by four struts
  hinged in the middle, with springs. Pressed flat, the struts fold inward and
  the silk sides fold into concertina pleats; a tap or flick snaps it open
  (hence *chapeau claque*). A tired spring leaves it half-open: a leaning, lower
  crown with a crease ring around the middle.
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_hat),
  [Victorian Collections](https://victoriancollections.net.au/stories/a-snappy-collapsible-hat),
  [History on your head](https://historyonyourhead.com/the-gibus/)

## Takeaways for the drawing

1. **Sleeves: long, not short.** The first owner was taller, so on short
   arms the sleeve runs 15-25% of the arm length past the wrist, with a
   turn-back or a bunch of 2-3 folds at the cuff. On a limb that is too thick,
   switch to the Jingle tell: the sleeve stops 10-15% short and a band of bare
   hide shows.
2. **Button strain.** The coat front will not close over the width: the
   top button fastens, the next one or two pull into horizontal X-creases, and
   below them the fronts gap in a wedge about 10-20% of the body width, with
   the belly showing through.
3. **Shoulders too narrow, body too wide.** The coat's shoulder seam sits
   inboard of the true shoulder (about 80-85% of the body width), so the sleeve
   heads pull and there are diagonal drag lines from armpit to collar.
4. **Waist cinched with cord.** The trousers came from a bigger-bellied
   owner (or were let out) and sag, so rough cord hauls them in: draw a knot
   with two loose ends and the cloth gathered into 4-6 radial pleats above and
   below it, the waistband crumpled rather than flat.
5. **Trousers too long.** The leg is cut for someone 1.3-1.5 times taller:
   the hem pools in 2-3 folds over the feet, or is rolled once into a thick
   cuff about 8-10% of the leg height. The frock coat's skirts, which should
   fall to the knee, reach mid-shin or lower.
6. **Hat perch.** The gibus is too small for a wide head: it sits high and
   tipped 10-20° to one side, covering about 60% of the head width. One spring
   has gone, so the crown is 70-80% of full height, leans away from the dead
   side, and shows one or two concertina crease lines round its middle.
7. **Wear points.** Lighten (shine) or patch the elbows, cuff edges, coat
   hem, knees and trouser seat. Patches are square, a different cloth and
   tone, with visible stitch ticks. Fraying goes only on cuff and hem edges.
8. **Fading and mismatch.** Three owners, three blacks: the coat a faded
   green-black or brown-black (lighter at the shoulders and across the top
   from the sun), trousers a different dark with a stripe or check, the hat's
   silk the darkest and still a little shiny. Never one uniform black.
9. **Cut outlives fit.** Keep the tailored lines (lapels, tails, waist seam)
   crisp and correct, then place them wrong on the body: the waist seam sits
   too high on a short torso, the tails split over a wide rear. The gentility
   is in the cut, the misfit in where it sits.
