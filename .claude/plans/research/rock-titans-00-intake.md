# rock-titans · 00 intake research (2026-11-06)

Premise (D1, owner mid-interview): the four titans are built from the
final old ones' own shapes; scenery assets are at most filler. So the
research is about three things: how silhouette giants read as huge and
fight without becoming people, how held-step (stop-motion) motion sells
mass, and how a body made of parts breaks so that each part is already
the thing it becomes.

## Sources and what we take

- Silhouette animation (Reiniger, *Prince Achmed* 1926; cavac.at and
  wikipedia "Silhouette animation"; criterionchannel "Shadow Play").
  Cut-out figures lit from below, every reading in the outline alone,
  jointed at a few hinges. Take: a titan is a stack of flat cut-outs
  with 2-4 hinge points; a fight is read entirely from the outline
  changing between holds, never from interior detail. Matches
  art-style "Outline first" and "Holes, not lines".
- Harryhausen (Talos in *Jason and the Argonauts*, 1963; the skeleton
  fight; from memory, the search gave no reliable page). Stop-motion
  giants feel heavy because each pose is held and the next arrives
  late; Talos moves in long holds with a creak, and the fear is that he
  moves at all. Take: titans snap between poses with holds of 0.4-0.8 s;
  the first motion of a "rock" is the scare, so the first snap is
  small (a lean) and the second is the whole body.
- *Shadow of the Colossus* (2005; glitterberri developer interview,
  gamedeveloper "visual design"). The sixteen colossi stay distinct by
  body plan (biped, ox, turtle, bird), and size is sold by what is small
  next to them and by slow turning. Take: the four titans each get one
  body plan (a spire, a block, a ring-heap, a sheet) and never share a
  silhouette family; the old ones' 24 painters already sort into those
  four families (plan, Current state).
- *Princess Mononoke* (1997) Nightwalker and kaiju shadow fights (from
  memory). A giant whose feet are hidden by a skyline reads bigger than
  one shown whole; a fight behind a foreground reads as weather. Take:
  the option "titans behind the span, feet hidden, tops cut by the
  ceiling band" (Option map Q1).
- "Pulling Himself Together" (tvtropes) and the Iron Giant reassembly
  (1999, from memory): a body that breaks into pieces which are still
  recognisably its parts; the reverse (parts gathering into a body) is
  the AT&T "Building Blocks" SIGGRAPH spot. Take: whichever way a rock
  becomes a titan, each added piece is a whole old-one shape arriving
  or pushing out, never a blob that later gains detail; and when a
  piece breaks off it keeps its shape, so the chunk on the deck already
  is the old one (D1).
- Frozen II earth giants and *Noah*'s Watchers (2019, 2014; from memory,
  not searched): stone bodies with limbs read as rock golems, that is
  as people made of rock; the owner rejected "stickman features" once.
  Take: no arms, no fists; the fight is landslide contact (lean, crash,
  shear) or grinding, which keeps "creepier = non-humanoid".

## What applies here

1. Body plan per titan, one family each, no shared silhouette.
2. Held steps only; the hold sells the mass; first motion small.
3. Reading by outline: at 2000x1009 a piece must change the titan's
   outline to be seen; a piece inside the body reads only as a hole or
   a tone band (art-style "Third tone band").
4. Fights by contact and loss, not by limbs.
5. Pieces keep their shape through the break: the wreckage is the
   roster.

## Not taken

Gradients, glow, dust clouds on impact (colourless segment, no glow:
memory "hole burst: flat pour"); reassembly with tweened flight paths
(no in-betweens); any limb that swings.
