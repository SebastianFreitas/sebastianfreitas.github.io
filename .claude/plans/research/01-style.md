# Phase 1 · Style research and rules proposal

Researched 2026-09-25 (three web passes). Summaries are in our own words;
nothing here is to be copied as art, shapes or names into the site.

## Digest

### Dishonored 1/2 and Death of the Outsider (Arkane)

- **Silhouette first** (Kotaku, Mitton on inspiration:
  https://kotaku.com/dishonoreds-art-director-talks-inspiration-cut-levels-5953087).
  A character's mood should read from the outline before they speak or
  move. Proportions are pulp-magazine caricature: big hands and feet, gaunt
  sunken faces (https://www.shacknews.com/article/73538/the-unmistakably-english-art-of-dishonored).
- **Painted, low-noise surfaces** (80.lv, Dishonored 2 materials:
  https://80.lv/articles/asset-material-production-in-dishonored-2).
  Textures meant to look like an old oil painting. Detail stays at medium
  size, never fine. Wear is placed selectively. "Crazy shapes" were pushed.
- **Crisp tailoring on rough bodies** (The Art of Dishonored 2 review:
  https://brutalgamer.com/2016/11/21/the-art-of-dishonored-2/). Cloth
  edges are clean, hands and faces weathered. Rank reads from the costume;
  the body tells the story. Warm window light against cool grey alleys.
- **Class as height** (Dunwall architecture:
  https://videogamearchitecture.wordpress.com/2016/06/20/dishonored-dunwall-a-fully-realised-artistic-vision/).
  The rich sit high and lit, the poor low and dark. Coastal fog everywhere.
  No seasons and no day/night cycle, so the place feels stuck.
- **The Void** (https://dishonored.fandom.com/wiki/The_Void,
  https://dishonored.fandom.com/wiki/Whales). An endless dark with broken
  lumps of real places hanging frozen in it, and huge slow things drifting
  past. Black, lidless, highlight-free eyes on the god figure
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Outsider_(Dishonored)).
- **Death of the Outsider** (https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-story-behind-the-design-of-i-dishonored-death-of-the-outsider-i-).
  A cut Void house that changed with the chaos level; a cult that saw into
  the Void through a dead god's eye.

### Control (2019) and Control Resonant (2026)

- **The sequel is "Control Resonant"**, not "Resonance"
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_Resonant,
  https://www.pcgamer.com/games/an-otherworldly-algorithm-is-affecting-manhattan-in-control-resonant-which-is-why-there-are-giant-floating-heads-and-everything-looks-like-that/).
  It launched on 2026-09-24. An alien algorithm rewrites a sealed-off
  Manhattan: streets fold upward, towers refract away, bodies float and
  giant heads hang in the sky. That is the closest outside picture of our
  "tier 3 reaches tier 1 only through something else".
- **Brutalist anchor** (https://www.gamedeveloper.com/art/the-real-buildings-that-inspired-i-control-i-s-oldest-house).
  The weirdness only works against a heavy, believable, muted backdrop,
  with a few chosen accents.
- **The Board** (https://control.fandom.com/wiki/Astral_Plane,
  https://unwinnable.com/2019/10/09/the-mumbling-of-control/). A higher
  entity shown as one perfect, still, black geometric shape over a white
  void. Its speech is subtitled as slash-separated alternatives, as if it
  were searching for our grammar.
- **The Hiss.** A single red accent against grey concrete, and possessed
  bodies hanging still in the air at the same angle.
- **Redaction.** Flat black bars over documents are the story's voice.

### Flat 2D games

- **Inside** (https://www.pixelfondue.com/blog/2017/9/11/inside-inspiration-in-minimalist-lighting-and-composition).
  Distant layers are fogged toward the background value, so depth reads
  as scale. Colour is kept for a few accents.
- **Limbo** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limbo_(video_game)). All black
  silhouettes, including the giant spider, which is mostly shown as legs
  coming in from off-screen.
- **Darkest Dungeon** (https://www.gamespot.com/articles/the-gothic-sensibilities-of-darkest-dungeon/1100-6424880/).
  Woodcut spot blacks with no dead grey. Every corruption flows from one
  cosmic source.
- **Hollow Knight** (https://medium.com/3d-environmental-art/the-art-of-hollow-knight-f4c05dda3882).
  Near-monochrome layers per area, with black foreground silhouettes
  crossing in front.
- **Gris** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gris). Colour is rationed and
  tied to meaning.

### Victorian illustration and Gilded Age dress

- **Lotte Reiniger** (https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/features/scissors-make-films-lotte-reiniger-creating-her-magical-animations).
  Black cut-card figures show clothes and manners through the outline, and
  through holes cut into the shape (lace, fringes), never lines drawn on it.
- **Mike Mignola** (https://bentobitt.substack.com/p/black-shadows).
  Big blocky spot-black shadows, almost no mid-tones; small nicks along
  the shadow edge make it sit on the form.
- **Edward Gorey** (https://www.theparisreview.org/blog/2011/01/04/the-coats-of-edward-gorey/).
  Tall, thin, still figures. His own grand fur coat over canvas sneakers is
  the joke we want: one grand piece that does not belong.
- **du Maurier / Punch** (https://victorianweb.org/art/illustration/dumaurier/pva95.html).
  Society satire through over-tall figures, posture and fashion.
- **Doré, London: A Pilgrimage** (https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/gustave-dores-london-pilgrimage/).
  Scale comes from rows of small repeated figures under crushing masses.
- **Mervyn Peake** (https://www.frieze.com/article/sinister-sensuality-mervyn-peakes-drawings).
  Each grotesque is one trait pushed to the limit.
- **Bloodborne** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodborne). Victorian
  gothic turning eldritch; the higher beings are seen only as shapes on the
  skyline at certain moments.
- **Failbetter / Fallen London** (https://www.failbettergames.com/news/five-and-a-half-things-i-wish-id-known-when-i-started-paul-arendt-art-director-and-co-founder).
  Victorian eldritch drawn flat; compose by silhouette first.
- **Dress** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_hat,
  https://www.victorianweb.org/art/costume/nunn24.html,
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_coat,
  https://fashionhistory.fitnyc.edu/1880-1889/,
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolman). Stovepipe and chimney-pot top
  hats; the bowler for city business; Chesterfield (knee length, velvet
  collar), Inverness (shoulder cape), Ulster (long, belted, caped, fur
  lined); astrakhan trim; spats; canes; monocles. Women in the 1880s:
  dolman mantles cut over the bustle, fur and tassel trim, muffs, and hats
  so tall they were mocked in print.

### Garments ranked by readability at 30–120 px

1. Top hat: 3–6× head height, a chimney-pot bulge or a lean, perched askew.
2. Very wide brim (wide-awake or plumed lady's hat): 2–3× body width.
3. Bustle: a shelf at the rear; on a crawler, at the wrong end.
4. Inverness cape: a stepped double hem, slipping off one side.
5. Huge shoes or button boots: 2–3× long, under a legless blob.
6. Cane: a thin vertical and a knob, held by a tentacle or far too tall.
7. Bowler: a dome and a small brim; reads even tiny, on a huge head.
8. Fur or astrakhan collar: a scalloped outline edge, made huge.
9. Muff: a floating cylinder that several tentacles share.
10. Long coat hem: drags on the deck like a train; empty sleeves hang past
    the "hands".
11. Spats: a light band at the ankle; put them on eight legs.
12. Muffler: two tails lifting in the wind (the cheap motion cue).
13. Monocle: a ring cut through the shape, best on an eye creature.
14. Veil: a scalloped edge over the eye area.
15. Watch chain: a thin arc; reads only above ~80 px (the tailored one).

## Proposed "Eldritch" section for `art-style.md` (NOT applied)

Phase 2 decides. **add** = new, no clash with a current rule.
**change** = bends or breaks a current rule; argued one by one in Phase 2.

### Eldritch beings (old ones, far realms, tier 3)

1. **add · Outline first.** Every eldritch figure must read as a solid
   single-colour fill: its kind and its garment recognisable from the
   outline alone. Check by filling it flat. *Why:* Dishonored's and
   Failbetter's shared rule, and the only way 30 px figures carry clothes.
2. **add · Holes, not lines.** Interior detail (monocle, lace, the gap
   between cape and body, buttonholes on the tailored one) is cut through
   the shape to the backdrop, Reiniger style. *Why:* detail without
   outlines.
3. **add · One trait, pushed.** Each dressed old one wears one or two
   pieces, and one of them is exaggerated past sense: top hats 3–6× head
   height, brims 2–3× body width, shoes 2–3× long. *Why:* Peake and
   Mitton; the owner's brief.
4. **add · Nothing fits.** Found clothes show it: the hat perched with a
   gap under the brim or tipped, sleeves and hems too long and dragging on
   the deck, a coat off one shoulder, garment scale that ignores the
   wearer, a piece on the wrong end. Only the tailored one is symmetric,
   snug and complete. *Why:* the brief; Gorey's mismatch.
5. **add · Crisp cloth, rough body.** Cloth edges are clean straight or
   gently curved polygons; the body under it keeps its jagged, lumpy,
   knuckled shape. *Why:* Dishonored 2's contrast makes the clothes look
   borrowed.
6. **add · Winter reads as mass.** Winter is shown by volume and edge, not
   texture: fur as a scalloped outline edge, collars turned up high,
   capes and mufflers doubling the shoulder line, snow as flat pale caps
   on the top edges (Phase 13). No fur texture, no hair strokes.
7. **add · Wardrobe palette.** Cloth is darker and less saturated than the
   wearer: coal black, slate, bottle green, oxblood, camel, with spats and
   collars as the one pale note. Metal (cane knob, watch) is dull ochre
   and never glows. *Why:* Dishonored's teal/ochre; the backdrop rule.
8. **change · A third tone band.** Allow up to three flat values per form
   (lit, mid, hard shadow) on cloth and on tier-3 shapes, as stepped
   bands. *Clashes with:* "flat fills" and one `litShade` split per volume.
   *Option:* keep `litShade`'s two tones and add the mid band as a second
   flat shape, never a gradient.
9. **change · Painted patches.** At most 2–3 medium, hard-edged patches a
   shade off the base on large surfaces (a coat back, a tier-3 flank).
   *Clashes with:* pure flat fill. Never fine noise, never soft edges.
10. **add · Spot-black shadow nicks.** The shadow side of a garment may
    have 1–3 small notches along its edge, still following the light from
    the left. *Why:* Mignola; it makes the shadow sit on the cloth.
11. **change · Black eyes.** Eldritch eyes become solid darkest fills with
    no highlight (only the tailored one might keep a monocle ring). *Clashes
    with:* today's `eyeDot` look on the roster; a visual change to all 24.
12. **add · Stillness is the uncanny.** Eldritch acting favours holding
    still where a body should move, then snapping to a new pose with no
    in-betweens; bodies hanging at the same angle; things frozen mid-fall.
    *Why:* Dishonored, Control; also the cheapest motion there is.

### Tier 3 (the sky shadows)

13. **add · Never whole.** A tier-3 being is never fully in frame: only an
    edge, a curve, a limb or a brim cut by the top of the screen. *Why:*
    Limbo, Bloodborne; the frame cannot hold it.
14. **add · Farthest value step.** Tier-3 shapes sit 1–2 flat steps off
    the backdrop (#0d1114), darker or lighter, never a mid or light value,
    in 3–4 stepped fog layers at most. Captions sit in the top band, so a
    tier-3 shape behind a caption stays within one step of the backdrop.
    *Why:* Inside's fogged far layers; "they do not know it exists".
15. **add · Absence as presence.** A tier-3 being may be drawn as a region
    where the far lights and motes are missing (cut out, not painted).
16. **add · Wrong parallax.** Tier 3 does not move with the camera's zoom
    or pan (or moves against it) while tiers 1 and 2 shift.
17. **add · Geometry against flesh.** Tier-3 forms are simple, large and
    near-perfect (arcs, straight edges, a huge brim, a lidless eye) set
    against the lumpy, organic tier 2. Our own shapes, never the Board's.
18. **change · Tier 3 ignores the light.** Tier 3 casts no left-lit
    `litShade` split: it is one flat value, or its shade falls straight
    down or toward the light. *Clashes with:* "light from the left". Only
    tier 3, and sparingly.
19. **add · Only effects reach below.** What tier 3 does appears only as
    effects in tiers 2 and 1: the chaos lurching in one step, blobs knocked
    sideways, far realms rearranged, motes all turning at once. Never a
    beam, a line or contact between tier 3 and anything below.
20. **add · Slow and wrong.** Tier-3 motion is very slow drift, against the
    wind or the march, or a single stepwise lurch; nothing bobs. Reduced
    motion: fully still, still visible.

### Tier 2 (the far realms) and accents

21. **add · One corruption hue.** One accent hue belongs to tier-2
    influence (to be chosen in Phase 4), used under ~10 % of the frame.
    It never tints non-emitters with a halo.
22. **change · Cold rift light.** Rifts in the chaos may emit a cool,
    flame-edged light that lights the nearest silhouette's facing side as
    one flat band. *Clashes with:* "light from the left" (a second, local
    light). *Option:* allow it only at `break` and `walk`.
23. **add · Floating debris.** The far realms may hold broken, frozen lumps
    of ground or wreckage with roots or pipes hanging under them, in the
    far value steps. *Why:* the Void's islands.

### Not proposed (considered and left out)

- Cross-hatching (Doré, Gorey, Darkest Dungeon): shapes only, never their
  rendering.
- Film grain, volumetric rays, fog gradients (Inside, Limbo).
- Redaction bars over captions: a copy change, so it belongs to D6 and
  Phase 22, not the art rules.

## Tensions for Phase 2

1. Rule 8 (a third tone band) and rule 9 (painted patches) against flat
   fills: the biggest style change. Proposed as cloth- and tier-3-only.
2. Rule 18 (tier 3 ignores the light) and rule 22 (cold rift light)
   against light from the left.
3. Rule 11 (black eyes) changes all 24 roster faces, not only the dressed.
4. Rule 14 and the captions: the top band holds the beat captions; tier 3
   must not drown them.
5. Rule 21's hue: today the chaos is grey and the brothers are red; red is
   also the Primordisentia's field (see the `walk` frame). Pick a hue that
   is neither (Phase 4).
6. CPU: rules 2, 6, 9 and 10 add shapes per figure; all static, so they
   go into the offscreen art caches (the owner's fans).
