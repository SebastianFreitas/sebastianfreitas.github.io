# 20 · The emergence (research)

How art stages a body coming out of material, and what that means for the
climb in `trade` (`js/genesis/genesis-oldones.js`, `place()` / `drawOldOnes`).

- **Michelangelo's Prisoners (non finito)**: the figures are half cut free
  from the block. The body is already whole inside the stone, and the stone
  line cuts across it. Takeaway: the old one is full size from the first
  frame. It surfaces through a lip, and it does not grow.
- **Rodin, The Gates of Hell**: bodies push out of a flat relief at
  different depths, each at its own moment, never in step. Takeaway: every
  being has its own step count and rhythm.
- **The golem / Adam from clay**: the maker's figure comes first and alone;
  the rest follow as a crowd. Takeaway: the tailored one (the gentleman)
  surfaces first, alone and slower, and the others follow once he is out.
- **Emergence in film and games** (the Alien chestburster, Dark Souls'
  lumbering beings climbing out of walls, Junji Ito): the horror is in the
  pauses. It holds, lurches up a notch, then holds again. Loose matter
  falls from the edge on every lurch. Takeaway: held steps with no
  in-betweens, and a few stone flakes drop at each snap.

## Decisions

- D28 holds: everyone is bare. Nothing comes out of the mass with them,
  since clothes come only at the womb.
- The climb is stepped:
  - n = 3–5 held steps per being (the gentleman gets 6);
  - full scale and full alpha from the first step;
  - the body is clipped at the lip, so it rises through the rim instead of
    scaling up.
- Each snap sheds three stone-coloured flakes from the lip. They fall for
  0.5 s. Their motion is driven by time and they are deterministic, so they
  are the same under reduced motion.
- Ceremony comes from the order:
  - the gentleman opens alone at 0.4 s and takes 2.4 s, with a wider push
    in the mass;
  - the others follow from 2.6 s, 0.2 s apart, in the old shuffled order.
