# fixthemothering 00: intake research digest

Brief: redo how ~24 small old ones (5-18% of its height) tend the colossal flesh
ball (half off the right edge, lit from the left) across swarm, womb seal, lens,
soul tongs, leaving, birth. Problem today: they stand, orbit, kneel; tools float
unheld. Format per source: link, **take**, **scene** (what it looks like in ours).
All in our own words; no art copied.

## 1. Tiny figures tending a colossal body

- Gulliver tied down (Swift 1726; Grandville 1842 plates), https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/GulliverTieDown
  **take:** a thousand thin cords pegged to the ground read as "many small hands" at once.
  **scene:** taut 1 px lines from ball rim to ground stakes and to figures leaning back on them; the lines ARE the touch.
- Shadow of the Colossus, https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-art-of-shadow-of-the-colossus-2-6-gameplay-and-story
  **take:** the body is the level; tiny climbers hang on fur/ledges, grip is a readable pose.
  **scene:** give the ball grip bands (ridges, folds); old ones hang from them with one arm up, legs dangling.
- Attack on Titan, walls of colossal bodies, https://attackontitan.fandom.com/wiki/Wall_Titan
  **take:** giant bodies turned into architecture; scouts on wires over skin.
  **scene:** later beats: the ball's surface gets built on, lines and hooks anchored into flesh.
- Nausicaa, the Ohmu's golden tendrils, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nausica%C3%A4_of_the_Valley_of_the_Wind_(film)
  **take:** gold threads as healing; the tiny one is carried by the giant's care, not the reverse.
  **scene:** the womb seal is gold thread laid by many hands, drawn as a sheaf of gold lines converging on the wound.
- Princess Mononoke, returning the head, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Mononoke
  **take:** two small figures offering a thing up with raised arms = responsibility, not worship.
  **scene:** a pair holds the gold seal overhead at the wound's edge, arms straight, a held step before it lands.
- Made in Abyss / Blame! (Tsukushi; Nihei), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Made_in_Abyss
  **take:** minuscule figures against vertical megastructure; scale sold by ladders and ledges, not size numbers.
  **scene:** one long ladder up the ball's lit flank with 3-4 figures spaced on it.
- Bruegel, Tower of Babel (1563), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tower_of_Babel_(Bruegel)
  **take:** 1000+ fingernail figures; scaffolds, a treadwheel crane lifting stone; every trade doing one task.
  **scene:** a treadwheel on the ground (two old ones walking inside) lifting a load up a rope to the lens.
- Bosch, Garden of Earthly Delights, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Garden_of_Earthly_Delights
  **take:** small bodies in and on huge organic forms (the tree-man, giant fruit) as the norm.
  **scene:** old ones half inside the lens cut, legs out, like figures in Bosch's hollow shells.
- Giger / Beksinski, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zdzis%C5%82aw_Beksi%C5%84ski
  **take:** flesh and structure fused; Beksinski's tiny figures under vast decaying forms, warm dust light.
  **scene:** mood only: ribs of scaffold that look grown; keep our flat bands, no texture.
- Junji Ito (Uzumaki, Gyo), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junji_Ito
  **take:** horror by repetition and slow wrongness, not gore.
  **scene:** in the exploitation beat, the same tending pose repeated on every figure turns care into a factory.
- Hollow Knight: Herrah and the Weavers, https://hollowknight.wiki/w/Weavers
  **take:** a colony that binds and seals with silk; a mother-queen sleeping as a seal.
  **scene:** the womb seal is woven: figures run back and forth across the wound trailing threads.
- Hollow Knight: The Hive, https://hollowknight.fandom.com/wiki/Hive
  **take:** workers tending a vast queen in a honeycomb.
  **scene:** hexagon cells on the gold seal; figures slotted into them.
- Bloodborne: Kos and the Fishing Hamlet, https://bloodborne.fandom.com/wiki/Kos
  **take:** a beached pregnant Great One; villagers harvest what lives inside it; an orphan tears out at death.
  **scene:** the closest precedent for our whole arc, including birth. The harvest beat: figures carry things away from the lens in baskets.
- Dark Souls: Witch of Izalith / Bed of Chaos, https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/The_Bed_of_Chaos
  **take:** a mother and daughters who tried to make a flame and became a bed of roots and flesh.
  **scene:** guilt: the old ones made the wound, so their first tending is repair.
- Scorn, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scorn_(video_game)
  **take:** tools that are organs; machinery clamped into flesh.
  **scene:** the soul tongs as a long jointed arm held by two figures, not a floating object.
- Inside: the Huddle, https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024472/Huddle-up-Making-the-SPOILER
  **take:** a flesh mass built from people; arms from a sack of bodies read as one creature.
  **scene:** the swarm beat: clinging figures along the rim become the ball's own grasping fringe.
- Little Nightmares, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Nightmares
  **take:** tiny figure, huge hosts; the Janitor's long arms; everything seen from knee height.
  **scene:** place a few figures low under the ball's belly, looking up, lit from the left.
- Ico, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ico
  **take:** holding hands as the whole mechanic; touch is the story.
  **scene:** one figure's hand flat on the flesh for a held step, the only still thing in a busy frame.

## 2. Care versus use

- Honeybee queen's retinue, https://honeybee.drawwing.org/book/queen-retinue
  **take:** 8-10 workers ring the queen, facing in, feeding, licking, removing waste.
  **scene:** a ring of figures all facing the wound, heads in; the ring breaks when care ends.
- Ant/termite nests, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Termite
  **take:** mass anonymous labour building a body-house around the queen.
  **scene:** lines of figures passing loads hand to hand along the ground to the ball.
- Midwifery and nursing, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwife
  **take:** hands low, supporting from below; basins, cloths, warm lamps.
  **scene:** birth beat: figures below the tear with arms out, braced to catch.
- Rembrandt, Anatomy Lesson of Dr Tulp (1632), https://www.mauritshuis.nl/en/our-collection/artworks/146-the-anatomy-lesson-of-dr-nicolaes-tulp
  **take:** pyramid of onlookers; one hand works, the rest lean in, none look at the body.
  **scene:** lens beat: one surgeon-figure at the cut, a stacked cluster behind leaning, heads angled away from the flesh.
- Whaling and flensing, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flensing
  **take:** cutting stage, long-handled spades, blubber peeled in blankets by winch, head to tail.
  **scene:** exploitation: planks jut from the ball, figures with long poles peel a strip that hangs down on a hook line.
- Cathedral builders in manuscripts, https://www.getty.edu/art/exhibitions/building_medieval_world/
  **take:** plank scaffolds, pulley and windlass hoisting stone to workers on top.
  **scene:** a stick scaffold against the ball's lit flank for the world-building beat.
- Treadwheel crane, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treadwheel_crane
  **take:** human-powered lift; walking inside a wheel is a perfect loop for held steps.
  **scene:** a wheel on the ground turning in 4 held steps; the rope rises in sync.
- Mount Rushmore carvers, https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/rushmore-carving/
  **take:** bosun chairs on cables from winch houses above; a call boy relays between them.
  **scene:** figures dangling on single lines against the flesh face; one figure on the top edge signalling.
- Shipwrights on a hull, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipwright
  **take:** ribs, props and shores; a curved body held up by many angled posts.
  **scene:** angled props under the ball's lower curve, each with a figure bracing it.
- Medieval doctors round a patient (Wellcome), https://wellcomecollection.org/
  **take:** bleeding bowls, urine flasks held up to light, bandaging; flat, symbolic poses.
  **scene:** a figure holding a vessel up into the light shaft, the others bent over.

## 3. Touch, tools and work with flat silhouettes and held steps

- Lotte Reiniger, https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/features/scissors-make-films-lotte-reiniger-creating-her-magical-animations
  **take:** jointed card puppets; action read entirely from the outline; hands and tools must sit clear of the body.
  **scene:** every tool has a visible gap between arm, tool and flesh; no tool overlaps a torso.
- Limbo, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limbo_(video_game)
  **take:** black silhouettes, depth by fog, ropes and levers doing all the story.
  **scene:** ropes and chains are the cheapest readable "contact" line; farther rigs lighter in value.
- Samorost / Machinarium (Amanita), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machinarium
  **take:** small creatures working odd machines with visible cranks, pulleys, pipes.
  **scene:** every tool gets a crank or handle and an operator; nothing moves without a hand on it.
- Kentucky Route Zero, https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1020596/Scenography-of-Kentucky-Route
  **take:** layered flat cut-out sets, theatrical lighting shifts to carry mood.
  **scene:** scaffolds as flat layers in front and behind the ball; lamp pools change colour per beat.
- Brothers Quay / Svankmajer, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brothers_Quay
  **take:** stop-motion that feels alchemical; screws and meat; repetitive mechanical tics.
  **scene:** held-step jitter on tools (tong jaws open/close in 2 frames) sells labour cheaply.
- Work lamps: lanterns on poles, a lamp per work site, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lantern
  **scene:** a warm dot and a small flat cone on the flesh at each work site marks where care happens.
- Stitches and poultices: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surgical_suture
  **scene:** a zigzag line across the wound drawn in steps as a figure moves along it; a flat pad pressed by two hands.
- Feeding lines: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feeding_tube
  **scene:** a hose from a ground vat up to the ball, a figure pumping a lever beside it.

## 4. Emotional arc: guilt, tenderness, exploitation, leaving

- Spirited Away, stink spirit bath, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirited_Away
  **take:** a revolting giant guest; the whole bathhouse hauls a rope together; disgust turns to joy.
  **scene:** guilt to tenderness: one figure starts, others join on the same rope, one by one.
- The Iron Giant, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Iron_Giant
  **take:** fear turns to care through small acts (feeding scrap, hiding him).
  **scene:** a single brave figure approaches first; the crowd follows its example.
- Where the Wild Things Are (Jonze 2009), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where_the_Wild_Things_Are_(film)
  **take:** a pile of monsters sleeping together as affection; the ending is a quiet goodbye.
  **scene:** tenderness: figures resting against the flesh, leaning their backs on it.
- Ghibli crowds (Mononoke's Irontown, Nausicaa's valley), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studio_Ghibli
  **take:** crowd mood shown by posture and spacing: bunched and still = awe; spread and busy = work.
  **scene:** we control mood by density and facing alone, cheap at 24 figures.
- Kos again: worship becomes harvest, https://bloodborne.fandom.com/wiki/Kos_Parasite
  **scene:** exploitation read: figures turn their backs to the ball, carrying loads out of frame left.
- Leaving: Gulliver's cords cut; whaling ships sailing from a carcass
  **scene:** figures untie lines and walk left; lines go slack and drop in held steps; scaffold stands empty for the birth.

Mood by posture (our rule of thumb):
- guilt: heads down, facing the ball, spaced wide, slow steps.
- tenderness: close, touching, hands flat, leaning in, warm lamp pools.
- exploitation: backs to it, repeated identical tasks, hooks and planks, cool light.
- leaving: facing left, lines slack, tools set down on the ground.

## Options

1. **Lilliput rigging:** they bind and hold the ball with taut cords, stakes and figures hauling; care is literally tension. (Gulliver; Spirited Away's rope pull)
2. **Hive court:** a ring of attendants facing inward, feeding, grooming, sealing the wound in hex cells; the ring breaks when they turn to harvest. (honeybee retinue; Hollow Knight's Hive; Kos)
3. **Cathedral scaffold:** plank scaffolds, ladders, a treadwheel crane and lamps build up on the lit flank; every tool has an operator; later the same rig becomes a flensing stage. (Bruegel's Babel; Getty manuscripts; flensing)
4. **Bosun-chair surgeons:** figures dangle on single lines from the top edge, a call-figure signalling, with one Tulp-like surgeon at the lens and a leaning cluster behind. (Mount Rushmore; Rembrandt)
5. **Weavers' seal:** runners cross the wound trailing gold threads until a woven womb forms; threads later cut to leave. (Herrah and the Weavers; Nausicaa's gold tendrils)
6. **Huddle fringe:** the swarm clings so tightly the figures become the ball's grasping rim, then peel away one by one as care cools. (Inside's Huddle; Shadow of the Colossus grip)
