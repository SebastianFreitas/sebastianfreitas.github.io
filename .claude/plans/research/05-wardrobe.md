# Phase 5 · Wardrobe casting sheet

2026-09-25. Who wears what, from `dress` on. Rules it obeys: D1, D2,
D8, D14, D15, D21–D23, D25 and the Eldritch section of
`.claude/rules/art-style.md` (one trait pushed, nothing fits, nothing
repeats, cloth darker than its wearer, colours from `GenEldPal`).

## Research digest (sources, and what we take)

- **Overcoats** ([Victorian Web, outerwear](https://www.victorianweb.org/art/costume/nunn24.html),
  [Inverness cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverness_cape),
  [Ulster coat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_coat)). Chesterfield:
  knee length, velvet collar, the gentleman's cut. Inverness: a deep
  shoulder cape over a sleeveless or wide-sleeved coat, a stepped double
  hem. Ulster: the cape stops at the elbow, a coachman's coat for long
  cold hours. Gladstone: short, edged with astrakhan. We take: the
  stepped hem as the cape's silhouette, the elbow-length cape as a
  second, shorter cape shape, astrakhan as a scalloped edge.
- **Hats** ([Victorian Web, hats](https://victorianweb.org/art/costume/nunn22.html),
  [Laird, the topper](https://lairdlondon.co.uk/blogs/journal/the-histories-of-the-topper)).
  Toppers reached ~30 cm by mid-century; the stovepipe is straight-sided
  and narrow-brimmed; the gibus (chapeau claque) folds flat on a spring;
  the bowler (from 1860) is a hard dome with a rolled brim; by the 1890s
  the bowler displaced the topper for day. We take: three distinct tall
  or odd hats (silk topper, stovepipe, half-sprung gibus) plus the
  bowler, so no two hats share a shape.
- **Women's winter dress** ([fashion-era, mantles](https://fashion-era.com/fashion-history/victorians/mantle-clothing),
  [FIT, muff](https://fashionhistory.fitnyc.edu/muff/),
  [poke bonnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poke_bonnet),
  [Click Americana, 1891 winter](https://clickamericana.com/topics/beauty-fashion/victorian-ladys-way-keep-warm-fall-winter-clothing-1891)).
  The 1880s mantle is long and slim with wide dolman sleeves over the
  bustle; muff, tippet and collar cut from one fur; muffs came tubular or
  flat, sometimes huge, with a pocket inside; bonnets carried plush and
  feathers. We take: the dolman mantle as a hump-shaped drape, the muff as
  a shared cylinder, the poke bonnet's forward brim, plumes as a flat cut
  shape.
- **Scarves** ([The Genealogist, mufflers](https://www.thegenealogist.co.uk/featuredarticles/discover-your-ancestors/periodical/20/history-in-the-details-scarves--mufflers-4721/)).
  Knitted comforters, sometimes in mock-plaid stripes, and later fur
  scarves. We take: two different neck pieces, a plain long muffler and a
  striped comforter, so they never read as the same asset.
- **Accessories** ([Victorian Web, accessories](https://victorianweb.org/art/costume/nunn12.html),
  [Vintage Dancer, men's accessories](https://vintagedancer.com/victorian/victorian-mens-accessories/)).
  Spats: stiff pale covers over the shoe top, a rich young man's mark.
  Monocle: upper-class and a little ridiculous by the 1880s. Canes:
  silver knobs or crook handles. Watch chain across the waistcoat. We
  take: spats as the one pale band, the monocle as a ring cut through a
  shape, the cane and chain only on the tailored one.
- **Tailoring** ([Frock coat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frock_coat),
  [Gentleman's Gazette, dinner jacket](https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/tuxedo-black-tie-guide/black-tie-tuxedo-history/dinner-jacket-debut/)).
  Every coat was cut to the wearer's measure; the frock coat was the
  formal day coat, knee length, closed, over a waistcoat. We take: the
  tailored one is snug and symmetric; the found suit is a frock coat that
  was cut for somebody else.
- **Second-hand trade** ([Victorian London, second-hand](https://www.victorianlondon.org/clothing/secondhand.htm),
  [Rag Fair, 1859](https://www.mernick.org.uk/thhol/ritchie_ragfair.html)).
  Old clothes piled at the sellers' feet, sold and resold down the
  classes until nothing fitted anyone. Phase 9's slab and Phase 19's
  old-clothes dealer draw on this.
- **Dressed creatures, past the list.** Grandville's *Un Autre Monde*
  ([Public Domain Review](https://publicdomainreview.org/essay/grandville-visions-and-dreams/))
  puts muffs and stockings on beasts and objects as satire: the garment
  worn exactly as a person would, on a body that cannot. Edward Gorey
  ([NYRB](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2018/05/10/edward-gorey-art-of-elsewhere/))
  gives huge fur coats and unseen faces a quiet menace. Dishonored's
  character art ([PC Gamer, Mitton](https://www.pcgamer.com/dishonored-3/))
  exaggerates features but balances the silhouette first. We take:
  sincerity (the old ones wear it seriously), and silhouette before
  detail.

## Totals

- 24 old ones: **17 east-goers** (the headless gentleman takes the
  colossus's slot and goes east, D2/D21), **7 west-goers**, bare for
  good (D14).
- East mix: **1 tailored full suit** (gentleman), **1 found full suit**
  (slab, owner's pick), **3 with two pieces** (tower, chime, comb),
  **12 with one piece**. The 7 west-goers are the "several with none".
- Every garment kind appears once. Origin of every first garment is
  *made* (D23); "how it came to fit wrong" is the maker's inexperience,
  and for the slab, the pattern it copied from another body.
- **Carers (D25, owner's pick):** mound, eye, chime. They stay, watch
  `birth` and `fight` at 55 %, keep their lamps lit at `leave`. The
  gentleman leaves to rule.
- **Wit:** high = makes a garment that suits its body; mid = makes the
  right thing, sized wrong; low = wears it wrong (wrong end, wrong way
  up). The low-wit ones get the funniest fit, never a sad one.

## For later phases

- **Eyes (Phase 6).** The code gives 15 of 24 an eye, but the rule says
  eyes are rare, each unique. Keep eyes on 6: eye, mound, tower, bundle
  (east) and strider, husk (west). Drop the rest; the slab's two front
  eyes become a single cut hole under the gibus brim.
- **Batches.** A (Phase 10): tower, mound, eye, chime, bundle, comb,
  mass: the large and frontmost, and all three carers. B (Phase 11):
  swarmling, prism, bloom, veil, blinker, roller, needle, knot.
  Gentleman is Phase 8, slab Phase 9.
- **Readability.** At the smallest sizes (needle, roller, blinker) the
  garment is the silhouette: the deerstalker's flaps, the boot and the
  bowler must read with the body filled flat.
- **Winter (Phase 13).** Tails that can lift: the mass's muffler, the
  chime's comforter, the bloom's ribbons, the deerstalker's flaps, the
  Inverness hem. Snow caps: the topper, stovepipe, gibus, bowler, gamp.
