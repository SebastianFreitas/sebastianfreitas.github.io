# Eldritch lore bible

The world behind the genesis cutscene's old ones, the bridge and the sky
shadows. Written 2026-09-25 (plan phase 3,
`.claude/plans/genesis-eldritch.md`; research in
`.claude/plans/research/03-lore.md`). Art rules live in
`.claude/rules/art-style.md` "Eldritch"; this file is what is *true*, so
the art and any copy can agree. Nothing here goes on the site verbatim:
it is a reference for painters and writers.

**Scope.** Eldritch means the tiers, the old ones and what lives on the
bridge. Nothing born of the Primordisentia's womb is eldritch: souls,
Rex, Obrokxus and his brothers, the five gods, the mainland and its
peoples. They appear here only where the eldritch touch them.

## The record is blind

The cutscene's captions are a record written by the soul kind, in
tier 1. It tells what tier 1 could learn and no more. So:

- It never names tier 3, never hints at it, never knows to. When the
  record says "what came out of it we cannot name" (`break`), it means
  the chaos; the reader who has seen the sky may take it further.
- It calls Obrokxus and Rex "first born" (`birth`). They are the first
  born of the womb. Tier 3 was born long before, and the record cannot
  know it.
- It says of the west-goers "nothing is written". That is the record's
  limit, not the world's.

Rule for all copy: **the narrator is tier 1.** Captions stay as they are
(Decision D6); any new text (beacon notes, readings) speaks from inside
the same blindness.

## Order of things

1. **The point.** Everything pressed into one place with no time.
2. **The span.** Out of the dark comes the bridge, cold and straight: the
   first law. It is tier 2's floor before anything lives on it.
3. **The breaking.** The point breaks. **The first thing out is tier 3**,
   before the chaos, before any element. It is the part the record
   "cannot name". It rises and spreads past the edges of the void and is
   from then on the backdrop of everything, too big to be seen as a thing.
4. **What had no body.** The elements: dust, air, wind, sound, colour,
   light. They are the smallest old ones, minds too thin to hold a shape
   (D10). They split three ways: a third **sinks** and becomes the sea at
   the bottom of the frame, over tier 1; a third **settles** on the
   bridge and becomes its weather and its dust; a third **rises** into the
   air and becomes the drift between the bridge and the sky.
5. **Matter.** Flesh and stone and both. Small lives crawl out and die.
6. **The old ones wake.** The matter clumps; almost nothing in it wakes;
   what wakes climbs out one by one. These are the old ones of the
   cutscene: tier-2 beings, the first with bodies that hold.
7. **One side.** They split west and east. The record follows the east.
8. **The Primordisentia**, the hunger, the guilt, the womb. Reshaping
   the womb, the east-goers learn from what they hold: they are
   inspired, change their own bodies and begin to wear clothes (D15).
9. **The work inside.** The Primordisentia is infinite souls devouring
   each other. The old ones help it: they build universes inside it,
   made and not conjured, force the souls apart, take pieces and remake
   souls, and send them through life after life until they rediscover
   magic, the soul kind's own power (D16).
10. **The leaving.** Once the cycle runs, the old ones go. They live
    their own lives and rule their own civilizations all over the void.
    From here on the story belongs to the soul kind, and the eldritch
    become the weather it lives in.

## The three tiers

| | Tier 1 | Tier 2 | Tier 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What** | The mortal realm | The bridge and all that lives on it | Things bigger than the void |
| **On screen** | Under the sea at the bottom of the frame; the mainland and Rex stand above it | The deck, the piers, the span; the old ones walk it | The top of the frame, behind the far realms; only edges |
| **Who** | Mortals: the soul kind's lives | The old ones, nest-builders, eldritch cities, the elements that settled | Unnamed. Few; how few is unknown and does not matter |
| **Scale** | Human | The bridge's thickness spans galaxies; the old ones are specks on it | Bigger than the void that holds the bridge |
| **Knows of** | Itself; tier 2 as horror and weather | Itself and tier 1 (as prey, curiosity, a source of clothes) | Unknown. It may not "know" in any sense we use |
| **Never knows** | Tier 3 | Tier 3 | — |

### Tier 1: under the sea

The mortal realm lies below the sea the sunk elements made. The sea is
the lid between tier 1 and tier 2: most of what falls from the bridge
lands in it, and most of what tier 1 knows of the eldritch is what the
sea washes up or lets through.

The soul kind is the exception that matters. Rex and the mainland stand
*above* the sea, not under it: born of the womb, not of the matter, they
reach up out of tier 1 and have tier 3's potential in them (D9). That is
why the eldritch fear and covet them, and why the Primordisentia's
children matter to the whole order. The record does not know this
either; it only knows that Rex "burned like a new star".

### Tier 2: the bridge

The bridge is the first law made solid. Its thickness alone spans
galaxies; the void's far realms (the dim blobs and far lights in the
cutscene's backdrop) are partly its underside and piers seen from very
far off. Scale shows only through life on it (D11):

- **Nests** on the piers and under the deck, built like birds' nests out
  of whatever fell there: bones of matter, shards of the breaking, lost
  cloth.
- **Cities** grown round the span, eldritch and tiny against it, each a
  crust of beings that stayed.
- **The weather** of the bridge is the elements that settled: dust that
  remembers being light, wind that has opinions.

The old ones live here. They do not think of it as a bridge; to them it
is the world, and its ends are rumours. The Administration (site lore,
`js/depths/lore.js`) is tier-2 high society that tries to order it.

### Tier 3: the sky

Born first, bigger than the void. It is not a place but a presence: the
backdrop behind the backdrop. Nothing in tier 1 or 2 can perceive it,
because of scale, not secrecy: an old one looking up sees the chaos and
the far lights; the tier-3 being is the reason those lights are missing
in one region, and the old one's eye has no way to read an absence that
big as a shape. As a cell cannot see the body, a speck on the bridge
cannot see what the void is small inside.

Tier 3 is **not evil and not a god**. It does not plan against anyone,
and may not be aware at all: its acts may be reflexes, as a body turns in
its sleep. They are so large that they arrive below as nature.

Three things keep tiers 1 and 2 blind, and none of them is a guardian:

- **Scale.** What is seen of tier 3 from below is a slice, and a slice
  of something that big reads as landscape: the curve of the dark, a
  colour of the sky, a region with no lights.
- **It does not stick.** A being that glimpses tier 3 forgets it; what
  stays is a habit (looking up at a certain hour, a reason to hold
  still) with no memory behind it.
- **Seeing more costs.** The rare old one who starts to notice the tides
  turns strange: it stares up, stops dressing, stops trading, and the
  bridge's society drops it. It is never believed and never sure.

## How tier 3 acts: the indirect chain

Tier 3 never touches tier 2 or tier 1. It moves; something else follows.

```
tier 3 moves            (a brim tilts, a curve turns, an absence slides)
  → the void responds   (the chaos lurches, motes turn at once, far lights go out)
    → tier 2 feels it   (weather on the bridge, a city shaken, old ones restless,
                         a garment lost to the wind, a nest emptied)
      → the sea carries it down
        → tier 1 lives it as fate: a storm, a plague of dreams, a mad scholar
```

What the old ones feel, and what they blame it on:

- **Weather.** Cold that comes from nowhere; the chaos thickening like a
  front. This is why old ones dress for winter: they feel the draughts of
  tier 3 without knowing the door.
- **Tides and pulls.** Everything on the bridge leaning one way for an
  instant; motes and dust turning together. Old ones call it the span
  breathing.
- **Silences.** A band of the far realms going dark. Old ones stop and
  hold still until it passes (the uncanny stillness of the acting rules
  has a reason: they are listening for something they cannot name).
- **Urges.** A whole city deciding to walk east at once. The east-goers'
  march may be one: they think they chose.
- **Customs.** Tier 2 keeps habits nobody can explain: a season that must
  never end, processions along the span, a ball held whenever the far
  lights dim. They are rhythms kept in time with something vast, and
  nobody remembers the reason.
- **Relics.** Where tier 3 once shifted, the bridge is left changed: a
  stretch where dust falls sideways, a pier that hums. Old ones treat
  these as sacred or cursed and fight over them.

Feeling runs the other way too: tier 1's fear and longing rise through
the sea as tier 2's weather, as tier 2's moods rise into nothing that
notices. Every tier is another's climate and none of them knows it.

Tier 1 inherits the chain last and worst: the mainland's scholars who
"reached too deep into the chaos, and something reached back" (site
lore) reached tier 2. Nobody reaches tier 3.

## The old ones

Tier-2 beings that woke out of matter. Almost none are humanoid; most
have no body a garment was ever made for. Each is its own kind: no two
share a body, a garment, an eye (art rule "nothing repeats"), and the
lore agrees: **every old one is the only one of its kind.** There are no
races of old ones, only individuals, which is why they are old: they do
not breed, they persist.

- **Eyes** are rare. An old one with an eye has an eye no other being
  has; it is a trait, not an organ.
- **The smallest old ones** are the elements (D10): minds too thin for a
  body, now sea, weather and air.
- **West-goers and east-goers.** Both are old ones. The record follows the
  east; the west-goers are not lesser, only unwritten.
- **Descending.** When an old one descends from the bridge through the
  sea, it leaves a hole the Administration mends (site lore). The Void
  Vikings descend from old ones (site lore): their blood is old-one blood
  thinned in tier 1.

## Why old ones dress

**It started at the womb.** Only the east-goers dress: the old ones who
met the soul kind. While they reshaped the Primordisentia into a womb
they learned from it, and what they learned inspired them: they changed
their own bodies and began to wear clothes. The west-goers never met the
soul kind and never dress; they keep the bodies they climbed out of the
stone with. So clothing on the bridge is a mark of that meeting, a taste
caught from the soul kind like an accent.

Once begun, three things keep them dressed, in the order they matter.

1. **Warmth against the chaos.** The chaos is cold the way deep water is
   cold: it takes shape away. A body in it slowly forgets its outline.
   Cloth is made to hold a shape, so it keeps one; a coat is a wall
   against unmaking. That is why the clothes are **winter** clothes:
   fur, wool, capes, mufflers, collars turned up. It is also why the
   bodies under the cloth stay rough and lumpy while the cloth stays
   crisp: the cloth remembers its shape for them.
2. **Status.** On a bridge of individuals with no kin and no nation,
   rank is what you wear. The higher the hat, the higher the being. A
   garment that could only have been made (not found) proves access to
   makers: that is high society.
3. **Trophies.** Most clothes come from tier 1: taken from the drowned,
   washed up through the sea, pulled from a scholar who reached too far.
   To wear a mortal's hat is to wear a story of having been below. The
   old ones find mortals interesting the way we find beetles
   interesting, and the hats are the pins.

They never think of it as fashion. To an old one, a top hat is a tower
it carries, a bonnet is a nest, a muff is a room.

## Where clothes come from

**99 % never owned their clothes.** Every garment has a road:

- **Taken.** From a mortal, from another old one, from a nest. Taken
  clothes are the most common and fit worst.
- **Found.** Washed onto the bridge or dropped by the wind (the settled
  elements move a great deal of cloth). Found clothes are sun-bleached,
  salted, or missing parts.
- **Gifted.** Passed down from high society as a favour or a leash, the
  way a great house passed its cast-offs to servants. A gifted coat is
  finer than its wearer and says whose they are.
- **Made.** There are many tailors: in the eldritch cities, on the
  span, some high, some back-street. Most make for high society; a few
  cut down old things for whoever pays.
- **Traded.** The eldritch cities keep something like a rag fair: piles
  of cloth sold by weight, hats by height, and an old-clothes dealer who
  walks the span buying from anyone.

Every coat has a road, and fit gets worse at every step down it. Two
laws of the road:

- **The best comes down only after a death.** Gifts from above arrive a
  season late and never include the furs. Fine cloth reaches the lower
  bridge only when a high one falls, and then the whole bridge's
  wardrobe shifts at once.
- **Black is borrowed grief.** Much of the bridge's black is mourning
  wear that tier 1 cleared out as unlucky once the grief was over. Old
  ones wear it with no idea what it meant, and find it warm.

Nothing fits because nothing was made for these bodies: a hat perched on
a lump with a gap under it, a coat hung off the wrong end, one huge boot.
Wearers do not mind. Fit is a tier-1 idea.

**Tailoring is the mark of high society.** A tailored garment means
someone was willing and able to measure *you*: your particular, unique,
unrepeatable body. Among individuals who share nothing, that is the
highest luxury there is. Only high society has anything cut to size;
the rest of the bridge can tell at a glance.

## The headless gentleman

The one full tailored suit (D1, D2): tall, thin, immaculate, no skin
showing, and no head. A very tall hat sits where a head would be. He
replaces the colossus and walks east.

What is true of him:

- He is high society, perhaps of the Administration's circle, perhaps
  above it. He is the only being in the cutscene dressed for the cold
  *completely*: no gap for the chaos to reach in.
- He has no head because he does not need one: the hat is his head in
  every sense the bridge cares about. His outfit is his only face: to
  address him is to address the suit. Whether a head was ever there is
  a question nobody asks him.
- His warmth is fit, not bulk. Where the found coats of the lower bridge
  pile fur on fur, his is close and thin: proof that he has never had to
  make do.
- His suit fits because it was made for him. One of the many tailors
  made it; which one, he has never said, and nobody asks.
- He is the stillest of the old ones. When the silences come, he is
  already still.

The second suit (D1) is the opposite: complete, but found. Every piece
from a different owner, none of them his size.

## The soul kind and the eldritch

Not eldritch, and the eldritch know it. Born of the womb, bound in gold
by the old ones' first guilt, and raised through the old ones' work
inside it. From the eldritch side:

- The old ones remade the soul kind, but not its magic: the souls had to
  rediscover that themselves, over many lives, in universes the old ones
  built without any. Rex and the mainland reach *upward*, which nothing
  from matter does, and the old ones copied their clothes, not that.
- The tier-3 potential in the soul kind (D9) is felt by old ones as
  something wrong and attractive, the way a draught near a door is. It
  is why eldritch beings haunt the mainland's edges.
- Obrokxus's brothers are red because they are of the womb, not of the
  matter; they are out of this bible's scope (D5).

## Civilizations across the void

After the leaving, the old ones who met the soul kind found their own
civilizations all over the void: on the bridge, round its piers, far out
in the realms. Each is alien to the others, since every old one is the
only one of its kind; what they share is the habit of dress and the
tailors that serve it. The Administration is one such order; the eldritch
cities of Phase 19 are others. The west-goers' side of the span has no
cities that anyone has written of.

## Writing rules

- The narrator is tier 1 and blind to tier 3. Never name tier 3 in copy.
- Old ones are "old ones", lower case in running text; the site's lore
  notes use "Old Ones" as a title and may keep it.
- Say coherent or nomic, never "magic", for eldritch powers (the
  `womb` caption's Latin is the record's phrase, not a description).
- No names, places or terms from Lovecraft, Arkane, Remedy, FromSoftware
  or Games Workshop in the site. Ideas only.
- Every old one is unique: never write "an old one of the X kind" as if
  kinds were species.
